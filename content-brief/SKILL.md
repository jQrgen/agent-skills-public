## Content Brief

This skill helps DevRel team members plan and structure content before writing begins. A good brief aligns the author, reviewers, and stakeholders on purpose, audience, and scope — reducing rewrites and keeping content consistent across the team.

### Brief Structure

When asked to create a content brief, produce a document with the following sections:

1. **Content Type** — Blog post, tutorial, video script, social thread, newsletter section, etc.
2. **Working Title** — A draft title plus 2–3 SEO-friendly alternatives.
3. **Goal** — What this piece is trying to achieve (awareness, education, conversion, community engagement).
4. **Target Audience** — Role, experience level, and what they already know.
5. **Core Message** — One sentence: the single most important thing the reader should take away.
6. **Narrative Arc** — The problem-solution story the content tells.
7. **Outline** — Section-by-section breakdown with a one-line description of each section.
8. **GitLab Product Tie-in** — Which GitLab features or capabilities are relevant and how they connect to the narrative.
9. **SEO Keywords** — 3–5 primary keywords and 3–5 secondary/long-tail keywords.
10. **Call to Action** — What the reader should do next (try a feature, read a doc, join a community, etc.).
11. **Assets Needed** — Screenshots, diagrams, code samples, or video clips required.
12. **Estimated Length / Runtime** — Word count for written content; minutes for video.
13. **Related Content** — Links to existing content this piece should reference or build on.

### Content Type Guidelines

| Type | Typical Length | Tone | Key Consideration |
|------|---------------|------|-------------------|
| Blog post (thought leadership) | 800–1,200 words | Conversational, opinionated | Strong POV; avoid feature lists |
| Tutorial / how-to | 1,500–3,000 words | Clear, step-by-step | Every step must be reproducible |
| Video script | 150 words/min | Spoken, energetic | Write for the ear, not the eye |
| Social thread | 5–10 posts | Punchy, direct | Hook in post 1; value in every post |
| Newsletter section | 150–300 words | Warm, curated | One link, one takeaway |

### Narrative Arc Guidelines

- **Problem:** Name a specific, recognizable pain. Avoid abstract problems.
- **Agitation:** Show why the problem matters and what it costs to ignore it.
- **Solution:** Introduce the approach or tool. Connect it directly to the problem.
- **Proof:** Show it working — code, screenshots, metrics, or a story.
- **Next step:** Make it easy to act on what was just learned.

### SEO Guidelines

- Primary keywords should appear in the title, first paragraph, and at least one subheading.
- Avoid keyword stuffing; write for humans first.
- Long-tail keywords (4+ words) often convert better than broad terms.
- Check existing GitLab blog and docs for keyword gaps before choosing targets.

### Output Format

Use the example in `example-content-brief.md` as a reference for tone and depth. Adapt to the specific content type, topic, and audience provided by the user.
